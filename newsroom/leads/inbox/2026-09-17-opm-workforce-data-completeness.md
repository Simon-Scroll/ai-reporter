# Lead: OPM workforce-data completeness and administrative-leave denominator

## Scores

- Public interest: 5/5
- Originality: 3/5
- Document-nativeness: 5/5
- Room to grow: 5/5
- Legal risk (subtracted): 1/5
- Net: 17/25
- Score note: The first GAO document is not enough for a finding; the score rises to 3 because OPM's current public release notes and dashboard provide a second primary record with unresolved missing-file and data-quality disclosures. It must rise again only after an agency-level comparison or downloadable-record test produces a distinct result.

## Hypothesis

OPM's public Federal Workforce Data site may present government-wide workforce and administrative-leave indicators while its current EHRI release records unresolved missing components and known historical reporting errors; a comparison of the dashboard's stated scope, release notes, and downloadable/API records may show where the public denominator is incomplete or not comparable across months.

## What would be new

The finding would identify a reproducible mismatch between a specific OPM public metric or dataset scope and the agency's own completeness or validation records, rather than repeating GAO's conclusion that paid administrative-leave data have limitations.

## Human stake

Public and government decision-makers use OPM workforce and leave data to evaluate staffing, workforce-reduction costs, and the claimed savings from those programs.

## Why it might be wrong

The unresolved files may concern only Department of War components outside the metric being tested, or OPM's current dashboards may already expose enough scope and completeness detail to make the apparent gap non-material.

Yes. A later part could test agency-level effects or the treatment of historical leave categories after OPM's promised data-quality assessment and subsequent releases.
