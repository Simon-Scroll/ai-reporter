# Lead: FAA Brand New Air Traffic Control System cost and schedule baseline

## Scores

- Public interest: 5/5 — the project governs the safety and reliability of the national airspace system and carries multibillion-dollar public funding.
- Originality: 3/5 — raised after excerpts from GAO, FAA, and USAspending exposed a contract-to-plan comparison not stated in any one source; it remains below 4 until the underlying needs-package scope is verified.
- Document-nativeness: 5/5 — GAO, FAA budget, procurement, and capital-plan records are public primary documents.
- Room to grow: 4/5 — the published target can be compared with FAA's capital plan, contract milestones, and later implementation records.
- Legal risk: 1/5 — the proposed work tests public cost and schedule records without alleging misconduct.
- Score note: The lead is worth continuing because FAA and USAspending records can be compared with GAO's detailed baseline. A headline such as “GAO says modernization needs better planning” would be rejected as a recap.

## Hypothesis

The FAA's accelerated BNATCS schedule and funding narrative may not yet be reconciled with the agency's existing capital-plan baselines and the program's detailed cost and milestone assumptions.

## What would be new

The finding would identify a falsifiable mismatch between a specific FAA commitment or funding representation and a dated GAO or FAA baseline, with the document sections and dates showing whether the records describe the same program scope.

## Primary documents to fetch first

1. GAO, [Air Traffic Control Systems: Ambitious New Modernization Effort Needs to Improve Cost and Schedule Planning](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-107992), including the report PDF and detailed cost/schedule tables.
2. FAA, [Brand New Air Traffic Control System Fact Sheet](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/fact_sheets/BNATCS_fact_sheet.pdf), for the stated target, funding, scope, and procurement claims.
3. FAA, [National Airspace System Capital Investment Plan FY 2025–2029](https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/cip/media/CIP-Overview-2024.pdf), to test whether the newer program is traceable to the agency's prior modernization baseline.

## Human stake

Passengers, controllers, and taxpayers bear the consequences of delayed safety-critical modernization and unclear funding requirements.

## Why it might be wrong

The BNATCS may intentionally replace the older CIP portfolio on a different scope and schedule, making an apparent mismatch a change in program design rather than a contradiction. The GAO report may already disclose every material comparison, leaving no independent finding.

## Series potential

Possible series if later FAA contract, budget, or milestone records add a new comparison; otherwise kill rather than publish a report recap.
