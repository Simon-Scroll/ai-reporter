# Adversarial review — 2026-10-26

## Review scope

Read the draft, claim ledger, evidence log, all cited NIH evidence records, and the cited GAO record. The review tests whether the proposed comparison is original, reproducible, and bounded.

## Claims that survive

- **C1 survives.** The three NIH briefs independently state their as-of dates and the contacted-institution denominators: 255, 263, and 273. Each report defines the population as cases in which NIH staff contacted institutions. The scientist totals are also stated in each report.
- **C2 survives with a scope limit.** The outcome and repayment figures are in the cited tables and report sections. The draft does not add overlapping categories or imply that the totals represent the same cohort at each cutoff.
- **C3 survives.** The Decision Matrix and May 2026 NIH update describe the contact/mitigation logic. The matrix expressly says most allegations close internally without outreach; the article uses that statement only to explain why incoming allegations and contacted cases are different populations.
- **C4 survives as a bounded negative finding.** The reviewer checked the public reports index, current aggregate page, three briefs, Decision Matrix, handling page, and May 2026 update. None provides a row-level historical crosswalk. The draft says “reviewed public materials,” not that no nonpublic or future record could exist.
- **C5 survives.** GAO's population and terminology are kept separate from NIH's contacted-case series; the article does not combine the denominators or treat disclosure violations as proof of foreign interference.

## Publication attacks considered

- The finding is not the source reports' own summary: it joins three time snapshots and tests whether the public record exposes the transitions between them.
- The 18-case increase is a subtraction across different cutoff dates, not a claim that exactly 18 new cases were opened. The draft explicitly says the records do not reveal whether cases were added, resolved, or reclassified.
- “Same top-level population” is limited to NIH's contacted-institution definition. The article acknowledges different as-of dates and does not claim the underlying cases are identical.
- No person, institution, country, or allegation is characterized as culpable. The article distinguishes allegations, contacted cases, and outcome measures.

## Claims that die

- None. No sentence should be broadened to say NIH's data are false, incomplete in an absolute sense, or evidence of unlawful conduct.

## Remaining gaps

- NIH has not published a case-level crosswalk in the public materials reviewed; a future semi-annual update could resolve the boundary.
- The three snapshots cannot establish whether changes reflect new cases, resolved reviews, or category reclassification.
- GAO's broader population is contextual only and cannot validate the NIH series.

## Recommendation

**Draft / hold for cadence.** The comparison is original and ready for publication after the six-day interval clears, subject to a fresh cadence check and validation. Do not publish on October 26 because the latest article was published October 24.
