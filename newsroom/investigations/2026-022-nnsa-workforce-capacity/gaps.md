# Gaps

- The apparent 248-position difference is a subtraction of a FY2026 budgeted/requested FTE level from a Q2 FY2026 actual employee count; the documents do not establish that the numbers are measured on the same date or accounting basis.
- The FY2026 request includes 38 Savannah River Operations Office FTEs transferred from DOE Environmental Management. The GAO product page does not state in its public summary whether the 1,755 actual count includes the same transferred workforce.
- The request is a budgeted staffing level, not proof that NNSA was required to employ 2,003 people or that every vacant position impaired a mission.
- GAO reports occupation and office effects in the full analysis, but the public product summary does not expose enough detail to join those losses to the 2,003-FTE plan.
- No record currently connects the workforce gap to a specific missed deliverable, safety event, contract outcome, or statutory violation.
- NNSA's 2024 Year in Review says a Strategic Workforce Plan exists through FY 2031, but the public record reviewed here does not provide that plan's headcount schedule or a direct crosswalk to the 2,003-FTE FY2026 request.
- The Savannah River contract workforce-plan requirements describe contractor staffing and skills, not the federal workforce denominator. The FY2025 Stockpile Stewardship and Management Plan confirms that federal and management-and-operating workforces are distinct.

## Decision required

The distinct records now support a labeled public-record comparison: NNSA's 2,003-FTE forward plan and GAO's 1,755-employee Q2 actual are joinable at the federal-workforce level, but the public materials reviewed do not reconcile the difference or disclose the treatment of the 38 transferred Savannah River positions. Write the gap without calling it a staffing shortfall. Do not claim mission failure, a statutory violation, or a safety consequence.
