# Adversarial review — 2026-10-18

## Claims that survive

- **C1:** The GAO product's “What GAO Found” locator supports the 1,991 peak, 1,755 Q2 FY2026 count, and 236-employee reduction.
- **C2:** The NNSA FSE request's “Highlights of the FY 2026 Request” supports 2,003 federal FTEs and the 38 Savannah River Operations Office FTEs transferred from Defense Environmental Cleanup.
- **C3:** The NNSA Year in Review's “NNSA WORKFORCE” section supports the Strategic Workforce Plan's FY2031 horizon and its role as the basis of the staffing plan.
- **C4:** The FY2025 SSMP's “Workforce Retention” appendix distinguishes the FSE-funded federal workforce from management-and-operating partners. The SRS contract record separately supports the contractor workforce-plan description.
- **C5:** The draft limits the reconciliation claim to the official records reviewed. It does not claim that no internal record exists.
- **C6:** The draft expressly rejects a staffing-shortfall, mission-failure, statutory-violation, or safety-consequence conclusion.

## Claims that die or require wording limits

- Do not describe 2,003 as an actual staffing requirement or say that 248 positions were vacant. The draft correctly calls it a forward-looking request and an arithmetic difference.
- Do not say the GAO count excludes or includes the 38 Savannah River positions. The public materials reviewed do not establish that treatment; the draft must retain “do not say whether.”
- Do not use the SRS contractor workforce plan as evidence of federal staffing. It is context showing why contractor records cannot reconcile the federal denominator.
- Do not turn GAO's 11.9 percent reduction into the article's finding. The article uses the underlying count only as one side of a cross-document comparison.

## Remaining gaps

- The public materials reviewed do not expose NNSA's Strategic Workforce Plan, a federal office-by-office FY2026 staffing schedule, or an official crosswalk between the GAO actual count and the budget request.
- The source set does not establish whether the 38 transferred Savannah River positions were present in the GAO Q2 count.
- The comparison cannot establish vacancies, hiring delays, mission effects, noncompliance, or safety consequences.

## Recommendation

**Publish with the current scope and labels.** The finding is the joinable but unreconciled relationship between a forward staffing plan and a later actual federal headcount. It is not a recap of GAO's workforce-reduction finding, and the article identifies the missing reconciliation record rather than inferring agency failure.
