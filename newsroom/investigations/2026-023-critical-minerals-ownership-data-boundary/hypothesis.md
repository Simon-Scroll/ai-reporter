# Hypothesis

BLM's public mining-record system exposes case, operator, mine-plan, and mineral identities in at least one public geospatial layer, but not the ownership and citizenship fields needed to connect statutory claimant eligibility to foreign-parent ownership for critical-mineral operations on federal land.

The first two primaries establish adjacent but not identical populations:

- GAO-27-108196 began with 236 business-entity claimants in BLM data for approved hardrock operations, then selected a nongeneralizable sample of 15 after matching against OpenCorporates and other sources. Seven of those 15 did not have evidence of U.S. incorporation in the records GAO reviewed.
- GAO-27-108094 reviewed 43 U.S.-incorporated companies that were claimants or operators associated with 59 identified critical-mineral mines on BLM-managed federal lands. GAO determined ownership for 32 and found 14 subsidiaries of foreign parents.

The October 21 BLM geospatial test supplied a stable case serial, business-account label, commodity, and operator relationship for one public mining-plan record. It did not supply citizenship or parent ownership. The two GAO populations still cannot be treated as overlapping or reconciled as counts, but the public-record boundary is now testable and bounded rather than merely hypothetical.
