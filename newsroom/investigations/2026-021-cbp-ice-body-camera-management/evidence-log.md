# Evidence log

## E1 — GAO-26-108157, body-camera program management

- URL: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108157
- Retrieved: 2026-10-15
- Locator: “What GAO Found”; “Why GAO Did This Study”; “Recommendations for Executive Action.”
- Captured excerpt: GAO says CBP and ICE had not updated their program plans since 2022 and 2024, respectively, so the plans did not account for changes including increased camera deployments and a 2025 pause. GAO also says most offices had not developed specific operational guidance and lists seven open recommendations, including updated plans and office guidance.
- Why it matters: Establishes the audit finding and the distinct plan/guidance records that the directives must be compared against; it is not, by itself, a new finding.

## E2 — ICE Directive 19010.3, Body Worn Camera

- URL: https://www.ice.gov/doclib/foia/policy/19010.3.pdf
- Retrieved: 2026-10-15
- Locator: Sections 2.2–2.3; sections 4.1, 4.2, and 4.5.
- Captured excerpt: Until cameras are issued enterprise-wide, the directive applies only where camera use and responsibilities have been implemented, and enterprise-wide implementation depends on appropriated funding. It assigns the Office of Firearms and Tactical Programs Program Management Office responsibility for managing and overseeing the ICE BWC Program and implementing requirements, while field officials are assigned implementation and compliance duties.
- Why it matters: Supplies the current policy-layer assignment and the explicit boundary between enterprise-wide policy and implemented locations.

## E3 — CBP Directive 4320-030B, Incident-Driven Video Recording System

- URL: https://www.cbp.gov/sites/default/files/2025-05/CBP-Directive-4320-030B-IDVRS-signed-508.pdf
- Retrieved: 2026-10-15
- Locator: Sections 4.1–4.2 and 7.1–7.2.
- Captured excerpt: The directive covers CBP offices and says it is overarching guidance that does not replace office-level SOPs; offices must issue SOPs before deployment. It assigns the Commissioner overall policy, acquisition, requirements, audit, and quality-assurance responsibility, while component leaders must update SOPs, monitor deployment, and ensure recorded data are audited.
- Why it matters: Supplies a second component’s policy-layer assignments and an explicit requirement for office-level procedures, allowing the investigation to test whether plans and local guidance are separate unresolved layers.
