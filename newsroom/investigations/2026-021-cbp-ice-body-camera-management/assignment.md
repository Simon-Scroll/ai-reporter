# Assignment: CBP and ICE body-camera plans versus current operating directives

## Scores

- Public interest: 5/5
- Originality: 3/5
- Document-nativeness: 5/5
- Room to grow: 4/5
- Legal risk (subtracted): 1/5
- Net: 16/25
- Score note: GAO's management finding is not itself the story. The current ICE and CBP directives provide independent records for testing whether policy-level assignments and local operating guidance actually update the program plans GAO found stale.

## Hypothesis

CBP and ICE have current body-camera directives that assign program-management, training, and local operating-guidance duties, but the public record may not show that those duties produced updated program plans or office-level guidance after deployment expanded and paused.

## What would be new

The investigation would identify a specific, reproducible boundary between a current directive, a program management plan, and field-office guidance, without claiming that a missing public file proves noncompliance.

## Primary documents to fetch first

2. ICE Directive 19010.3, [Body Worn Camera](https://www.ice.gov/doclib/foia/policy/19010.3.pdf)
3. CBP Directive 4320-030B, [Incident-Driven Video Recording System](https://www.cbp.gov/sites/default/files/2025-05/CBP-Directive-4320-030B-IDVRS-signed-508.pdf)

## Human stake

Body-camera rules govern what federal officers record during encounters with the public and how footage is stored, reviewed, and released.

## Why it might be wrong

The directives may be only policy frameworks, while updated plans or office guidance may exist in non-indexed agency records; GAO's review period may also predate later implementation changes.

## Series potential

Yes. A later part could test the status of the seven GAO recommendations against agency updates or subsequent oversight records.
