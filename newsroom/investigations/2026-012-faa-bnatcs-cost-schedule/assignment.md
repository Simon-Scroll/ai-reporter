# Assignment

## Hypothesis

FAA's accelerated BNATCS schedule and funding narrative may not yet be reconciled with the agency's contract-level commitments and cost baselines.

## Primary documents

1. GAO-26-107992, *Air Traffic Control Systems: Ambitious New Modernization Effort Needs to Improve Cost and Schedule Planning*:
   https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-107992
2. FAA, *Brand New Air Traffic Control System Fact Sheet*:
   https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/fact_sheets/BNATCS_fact_sheet.pdf
3. FAA, *National Airspace System Capital Investment Plan for Fiscal Years 2025–2029*:
   https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/cip/media/CIP-Overview-2024.pdf
4. USAspending, BNATCS integrator IDV and delivery-order records:
   https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_IDV_693KA726D00003_6920
   https://www.usaspending.gov/award/CONT_AWD_693KA726F00029_6920_693KA726D00003_6920

## What would be new

The finding would reconcile the GAO-reported negotiated integrator price and phase-cost baseline with the current public award obligations, potential values, delivery-order periods, and FAA's stated $12.5 billion funding narrative. It would distinguish a true scope or accounting mismatch from ordinary indefinite-delivery contract ceilings.

## Human stake

Passengers, controllers, and taxpayers bear the consequences of delayed safety-critical modernization and unclear multibillion-dollar commitments.

## Why it might be wrong

An IDV's potential amount may include options or unexercised needs packages and therefore may not be comparable to the negotiated integrator price. The BNATCS may also intentionally replace the older CIP portfolio on a different scope and schedule.

## Series potential

Yes, if later delivery orders, modifications, budget requests, or milestone records produce a new contract-to-plan comparison. Otherwise the desk should kill the lead rather than publish a report recap.
