# Assignment

## Hypothesis

DHS's 2025 grant review left a reproducible gap between component-level termination accounting and the public award records used to audit it, while FEMA's 2026 rolling-termination process may repeat the same weakness without a documented lessons-learned control.

## Primary documents

1. GAO-26-109097, *DHS Grants: Approach to Terminations and Pauses Disrupted Some Program Activities*:
   https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-109097
2. USAspending award and API records for DHS grants:
   https://api.usaspending.gov/api/v2/search/spending_by_award/
3. FEMA's public 2026 grant notices, standard terms, and documentation of the January 2026 rolling-termination process.
4. Relevant court orders and official DHS/FEMA award records for any selected program.

## What would be new

The finding would test the GAO table against current USAspending award records and FEMA's public 2026 grant-process documents, separating a real accounting or traceability gap from a search or timing artifact. It would not repeat GAO's conclusion that the review disrupted grant programs.

## Human stake

Grant interruptions affect disaster preparedness, public safety, state and local budgets, and the reliability of federal spending records.

## Why it might be wrong

USAspending search fields may not expose component program names consistently, and the GAO/DHS discrepancy may reflect different closeout periods or agency data unavailable in the public interface. FEMA may also document lessons learned in records not yet located.

## Series potential

Yes. A later part could trace one program from its notice of funding opportunity through termination, court intervention, and any restored award.
