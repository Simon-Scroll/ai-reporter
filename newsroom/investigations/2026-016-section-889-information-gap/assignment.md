# Assignment: Section 889 compliance visibility after the FAR overhaul

## Scores

- Public interest: 5/5

- Net: 17/25

## Hypothesis

The proposed Revolutionary FAR Overhaul would move Section 889 representations from the government-wide annual SAM process into each offer, while DoD's current DFARS still depends on annual SAM representations. That split may reduce the common, searchable compliance signal available to civilian agencies and purchase-card users even as future supply-chain prohibitions expand.

## What would be new

Map the current FAR representation mechanism, the proposed replacement, and DoD's retained annual-SAM requirement, then test whether the public record identifies how civilian agencies will access current compliance information after the proposed change. The work must identify a concrete public-record difference rather than restate GAO's recommendation to share lessons.

## Human stake

Federal agencies use contractor representations to screen supply-chain risks in purchases ranging from major contracts to small transactions; a less visible or non-comparable record could affect the reliability of those screens.

## Why it might be wrong

The proposed FAR provisions may be supplemented by public guidance, solicitation records, or a redesigned SAM process that preserves equivalent visibility. DoD's separate authority and mission-specific rules may also make the apparent divergence intentional rather than a government-wide gap.

## Series potential

Yes. A later part could test the post-reform representation process against a future semiconductor or other supply-chain prohibition.
