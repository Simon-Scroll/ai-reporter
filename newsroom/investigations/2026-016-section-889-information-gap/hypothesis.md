# Hypothesis

## Working hypothesis

The proposed Revolutionary FAR Overhaul would move Section 889 representations from the government-wide annual SAM process into each offer, while DoD's current DFARS still depends on annual SAM representations. That split may reduce the common, searchable compliance signal available to civilian agencies and purchase-card users even as future supply-chain prohibitions expand.

## Test

Compare GAO's description of the current representation and purchase-card workflow with the June 2026 proposed FAR text and the current DoD clauses. Identify whether a public replacement record, implementation instruction, or agency deviation preserves equivalent access for civilian agencies. Treat the absence of such a bridge as a public-record gap, not proof that agencies cannot comply.

## Boundaries

- GAO's recommendation is not itself the finding.
- The proposed FAR is not a final rule; describe it as proposed and date all conclusions.
- Do not infer noncompliance, intent, or national-security harm from a change in record design.
- Do not claim that civilian agencies lose access until the proposed text and any public implementation guidance have been compared.
