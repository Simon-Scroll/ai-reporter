## Hypothesis

DHS's 2025 grant review left a reproducible gap between component-level termination accounting and the public award records used to audit it, while FEMA's 2026 rolling-termination process may repeat the same weakness without a documented lessons-learned control.

## What would be new

The finding would test GAO's termination table against current USAspending award records and FEMA's 2026 grant-process documents, separating a real accounting or traceability gap from a search or timing artifact. It will not repeat GAO's conclusion that the review disrupted grant programs.

## Primary documents to fetch first

1. GAO-26-109097, *DHS Grants: Approach to Terminations and Pauses Disrupted Some Program Activities*: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-109097
2. USAspending award and API records for DHS grants: https://api.usaspending.gov/api/v2/search/spending_by_award/
3. FEMA's official BRIC implementation page and related 2026 grant notices: https://www.fema.gov/grants/mitigation/building-resilient-infrastructure-communities
4. Relevant court orders and official DHS/FEMA award records for any selected program.

## Human stake

Grant interruptions affect disaster preparedness, public safety, state and local budgets, and the reliability of federal spending records.

## Why it might be wrong

USAspending search fields may not expose component program names consistently, and the GAO/DHS discrepancy may reflect different closeout periods or agency data unavailable in the public interface. FEMA may also document lessons learned in records not yet located.
