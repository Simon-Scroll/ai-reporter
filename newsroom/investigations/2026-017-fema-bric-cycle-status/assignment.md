## Hypothesis

FEMA's restarted FY2024–25 BRIC cycle may have created a public status boundary: after the July 23, 2026 application deadline, the live OpenFEMA dataset lists 1,051 applications but every record remains "Submitted to FEMA," while FEMA's announcement describes a redesigned process intended to move money faster. The apparent delay may be ordinary review timing, a data-publication lag, or a reproducible gap between the program's promise and its public status record.

## What would be new

Compare the same cycle's funding announcement, NOFO process, and live application records, then test whether FEMA has published an award timeline, selection record, or financial record that explains the all-submitted status. Do not treat two months of post-deadline review as proof of failure.

## Human stake

The cycle covers $1 billion for projects meant to reduce disaster losses, while applicants and communities need a reliable public record of whether and when those funds are awarded.

## Why it might be wrong

FEMA may be in a normal eligibility or technical review period, and the public API may lag FEMA GO or omit records that are not yet publishable. GAO's older-cycle findings may not transfer to the restarted cycle.

## Series potential

Yes. A later part could compare selected applications with obligations and payments, or test whether the new review changes actually shorten award timelines.
