# Hypothesis

## Working hypothesis

After the July 23, 2026 deadline, FEMA's live OpenFEMA records show 1,051 FY2024–25 BRIC applications all still marked `Submitted to FEMA`, while FEMA described the redesigned cycle as intended to move money faster. The NOFO supplies post-review statuses but leaves selection and award dates to vary by award, so the public record may show a measurable status boundary without establishing delay.

## Test

Compare the FY2024–25 BRIC announcement and NOFO's stated review and award process with the current OpenFEMA record population. Test whether the public record supplies a common decision date or current-cycle selection result. Compare with GAO's older-cycle findings only where the populations, dates, and status definitions are explicit.

## Boundaries

- GAO-26-107774's findings about FY2020–2023 are not proof about the restarted cycle.
- FEMA's statement that the new process will move money faster is a program design claim, not an outcome.
- Two months between the application deadline and this retrieval is not by itself evidence of unreasonable delay.
- An API status is not proof that FEMA has taken no internal action; the dataset metadata says it comes from FEMA source systems and may contain reporting differences.
- The NOFO says both the anticipated funding-selection date and anticipated award date will vary by award.
- Do not infer waste, misconduct, or harm from an incomplete public status record.
