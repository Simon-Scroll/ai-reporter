## Locators and excerpts

- **pp. i–ii, Highlights and contents:** FAA had not completed risk and mitigation assessments or updated security documentation for spectrum-related threats; it lacked defined real-time monitoring for all spectrum-related threats. GAO says its review selected eight spectrum-dependent systems and made nine recommendations.
- **pp. 15–18, “FAA Has Not Developed Sufficient Threat Risk Assessment or Mitigation Reports” and “FAA Security Documents Are Not Aligned”:** seven of eight systems lacked separate detailed risk-assessment reports; four referenced NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 4; five did not include all required high-baseline controls; one had inconsistent impact categorization; and one had not completed its required annual assessment since 2023. GAO notes some corrective actions occurred after its review.
- **pp. 19–20, “FAA Lacks Real-Time Monitoring”:** FAA officials said Spectrum Engineering lacked continuous 24/7 monitoring and specialized tools for certain interference or spoofing threats, and FAA had limited visibility into some service providers and no monitoring of satellite providers.
- **pp. 41–43, conclusions and recommendations:** GAO says gaps remained in risk assessments, mitigation planning, security documentation, and real-time monitoring; Recommendation 3 calls for continuous monitoring of interference, spoofing, and jamming, and Recommendation 9 calls for a plan to strengthen authentication and data protection for ACARS and CPDLC.
- **pp. 47–50, methodology:** GAO reviewed authorization-to-operate packages for eight critical spectrum-dependent systems, collaboration documents, outage and incident reports, and FAA documentation. The system names were withheld for sensitivity reasons.
