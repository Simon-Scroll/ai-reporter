The lead has a second and third primary excerpt, so originality is above the single-document cap. The proposed finding is a time-bounded oversight and traceability comparison, not a restatement of GAO's conclusion.

## Hypothesis

After DOL paused annual affirmative-action-program certification and contractor audits, its August 2026 final rule removed the Section 503 7 percent utilization goal, disability self-identification inquiries, and related data collection while leaving written AAP obligations in place. DOL's current OFCCP page says the AAP certification period remains closed. The public record may therefore contain an unresolved measurement gap between the obligation contractors still have and the evidence OFCCP currently collects to monitor it.

## What would be new

The finding would compare the final regulatory text, the current OFCCP operating notice, and any post-September implementation or complaint records to determine whether DOL replaced the paused certification/audit trail with a comparable public oversight mechanism. It would not claim that contractors violated Section 503 or that the rule itself caused harm.

## Human stake

Federal contractors receive billions in public contracts, while applicants and employees with disabilities depend on enforceable and understandable nondiscrimination and affirmative-action obligations.

## Why it might be wrong

DOL may have a replacement monitoring process that is not linked from the public OFCCP page, or the effective-date transition may make the apparent gap temporary. The final rule may also make the old utilization data inappropriate rather than requiring a comparable replacement.

## Series potential

Yes. A later part could test the replacement process through complaint dispositions, contractor records, or a specific OFCCP/EEOC implementation decision.
