## The finding

**Fact.** GAO reported that DOL suspended annual Section 503 affirmative-action-program certification and contractor audits. For fiscal years 2021 through 2025, OFCCP received 3,063 Section 503 complaints and investigated 99; Table 1 records 309 complaints received and zero investigated in FY2025. GAO also said the written AAP requirement remained in effect, but that without the Contractor Portal there was no mechanism for contractors to demonstrate compliance to OFCCP. (C1)

**Fact.** DOL’s August 21 final rule, effective September 21 except for one delayed amendment, removed the Section 503 disability-inquiry requirement, related data collection, and 7 percent utilization requirements. It retained an annual written AAP obligation for covered contractors. (C2)

**Fact.** DOL’s current OFCCP operating notice says the agency administratively closed pending compliance reviews and took no further action on the November 2024 scheduling list. It also says the Section 503 and VEVRAA AAP certification period remains closed while OFCCP revises its processes and systems, even though the obligations remain in effect. The same page says Section 503 complaints held during an earlier abeyance were processed as appropriate and new complaints were processed normally after the abeyance was lifted. (C3)

**Fact.** DOL’s current complaint-process page gives individuals an online, email, or mail route for pre-complaint inquiries and employment-discrimination complaints. It says complaints must be filed within 300 calendar days of the alleged action. (C4)

## What the public record does not show

**Inference.** The complaint page describes an individual complaint process. It does not describe a contractor-level annual AAP certification or another way for a covered contractor to demonstrate its written program to OFCCP. The OFCCP operating notice, meanwhile, expressly says the AAP certification period remains closed. The pages reviewed therefore identify an active complaint channel without identifying a replacement public mechanism for the separate contractor-level AAP obligation. (C2–C5)

That is a traceability gap in the public record, not proof that contractors violated Section 503, that OFCCP stopped processing complaints, or that the final rule caused employment harm. The record does not answer whether an internal or future process exists outside the pages reviewed. (C6)
