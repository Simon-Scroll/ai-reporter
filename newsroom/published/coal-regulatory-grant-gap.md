## The finding

**Fact.** GAO’s permit table lists 1,565 permits in Kentucky, 935 in Pennsylvania, and 1,758 in West Virginia as of spring 2025. Together, those three states account for 4,258 of 5,247 permits, or 81.2% by desk calculation. GAO describes the three states as collectively managing over 80% of the national total. (C1)

**Fact.** The six OSMRE distribution tables show the following recommendation-to-distribution differences for those states:

| Fiscal year | Three-state recommendation | Three-state final distribution designation | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2021 | $40,015,563 | $37,540,156 | $2,475,407 |
| 2022 | $39,258,634 | $36,454,528 | $2,804,106 |
| 2023 | $42,801,862 | $36,534,536 | $6,267,326 |
| 2024 | $45,441,007 | $35,114,270 | $10,326,737 |
| 2025 | $43,430,020 | $35,141,444 | $8,288,576 |
| 2026 | $43,800,931 | $33,547,188 | $10,253,743 |

The recommendation and final-distribution figures are the sums of the Kentucky, Pennsylvania, and West Virginia rows in each year's table. Across six years, those recommendations total $254,748,017 and the final distribution designations total $214,332,122. The summed annual difference is $40,415,895. (C2)

**Fact.** Summing all six table totals produces $457,163,311 in recommendations and $387,090,000 in final distributions, a $70,073,311 difference. The three states therefore account for 57.7% of that six-table difference by desk calculation. This is a sum of annual differences, not an accumulated debt or money lost. (C3)

**Fact.** In FY2026, the final distribution designations covered 77.6% of Kentucky's recommendation, 75.8% of Pennsylvania's, and 76.1% of West Virginia's. Their dollar differences were $3,840,276, $3,778,176, and $2,635,291, respectively. (C4)

## What the calculation does—and does not—show

**Fact.** The OSMRE tables cite congressional appropriations for each final-distribution total. OSMRE’s Federal Assistance Manual says that a distribution designates funds but does not itself award or provide them; it also says available funds may be distributed equitably when they do not cover total needs. (C5)

**Inference.** The new comparison is geographic, not causal. It shows that the largest permit states account for most of the summed annual difference in the six public tables. It does not show that Kentucky, Pennsylvania, or West Virginia lacked the difference in usable funds, because the records reviewed do not establish whether other grants, state funds, fees, or later awards filled any part of it.

The denominator also needs care. The $70.073 million is the sum of six annual published table universes, not six observations of a fixed panel. Oklahoma appears in the FY2021 table but has no recommendation or final distribution in later tables, and OSMRE’s FY2022 footnotes exclude a Tennessee program-development grant and possible Oklahoma Indian-lands grants. Those separate exclusions affect the national six-table comparison; the three selected states appear in all six tables. The FY2026 table’s footnote also calls the final amount a “fiscal year 2025” distribution even though it cites FY2026 legislation; the printed total is used here as published. (C2, C3, C5)

The records establish a concentrated, reproducible difference between regional recommendations and final distribution designations. They do not establish that the difference caused a particular inspection, reclamation delay, environmental harm, or other operational failure. A state-level workload and outcome record would be needed to make that connection. (C6)
