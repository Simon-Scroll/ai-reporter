## The finding

**Fact.** GAO's public product for its NNSA workforce review says the federal workforce fell from 1,991 employees early in fiscal year 2025 to 1,755 through the second quarter of fiscal year 2026. (C1)

**Fact.** NNSA's FY2026 Federal Salaries and Expenses request supports 2,003 federal full-time equivalents. The request includes 38 additional FTEs at the Savannah River Operations Office that had previously been funded through Defense Environmental Cleanup after responsibility for the Savannah River Site moved to NNSA. (C2)

Those figures differ by 248 positions on their face: 2,003 requested FTEs minus 1,755 reported employees. That is a calculation, not a finding that 248 positions were vacant.

## What the records establish

The budget number is a planning level, not an on-board count. NNSA's 2024 Year in Review says the agency produced a Strategic Workforce Plan using workload drivers to identify federal workforce needs through FY2031, and that the plan serves as the basis of its staffing plan. (C3)

That distinction matters because the public records reviewed do not say whether GAO's 1,755-employee count includes the 38 Savannah River positions added to the FY2026 request. Nor do they provide a crosswalk showing how a forward-looking plan becomes the Q2 actual count. (C5)

The larger NNSA workforce also cannot be used to fill the gap. The FY2025 Stockpile Stewardship and Management Plan distinguishes the Federal Salaries and Expenses-funded federal workforce from management-and-operating partners. A separate Savannah River contract document requires the contractor to prepare its own workforce and skills-mix plan for current and future mission requirements. Those are different workforce records, not a federal headcount reconciliation. (C4)

## What this does not show

The comparison shows a public-record reconciliation gap, not a staffing shortfall. The documents do not establish that NNSA was required to have 2,003 employees on board at the time GAO counted 1,755, nor that the difference impaired a mission, violated a requirement, or created a safety consequence. (C6)

The next useful record would be NNSA's Strategic Workforce Plan or an official FY2026 workforce crosswalk identifying the denominator, the timing of the count, and the treatment of the Savannah River transfer.
