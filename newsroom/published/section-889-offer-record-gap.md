## The finding

**Fact.** GAO’s September report on Section 889 described the existing information trail: agencies use contractor representations in the System for Award Management (SAM), but officials told GAO they did not know how a proposed change would affect current information available to government purchase-card users. GAO also reported that representation data was unavailable for about 3,800 companies. (C1)

The current FAR-overhaul material answers part of that question, but not all of it.

**Fact.** The RFO FAR Part 4 model text keeps SAM as the primary method for collecting entity-level representations and certifications. It separately says that active SAM registration is not required for purchases under the micro-purchase threshold when a governmentwide commercial purchase card is used for purchase and payment. When registration is not required, the contracting officer is to collect certain information at the time an offer or quotation is received. (C2)

**Fact.** RFO FAR Part 40 relocates the Section 889 prohibition into the consolidated security part and requires the provision at 52.240-90, “Security Prohibitions and Exclusions Representations and Certifications,” in all solicitations. It requires the related 52.240-91 clause in solicitations and contracts. (C3)

That creates two distinct records: entity-level data in SAM, and a Section 889 representation tied to a solicitation or offer. The RFO pages are model-deviation text, not a final rule; HHS’s implementation record shows how at least one civilian agency is using them.

**Fact.** HHS instructs its contracting staff to use the RFO text and says: “System updates may lag policy updates.” It tells contracting officers to rely on representations from offers based on the solicitation and says entities cannot remove the displaced representations from SAM. (C4)

GSA’s own RGO-2026-01 memo is narrower. It applies to GSA contracting activities and procurements and directs the GSA workforce to use applicable RGO deviation language instead of the codified GSAR. (C5)

## What the comparison shows

**Inference.** For an ordinary civilian solicitation, the official record now shows a replacement compliance path: the agency asks for the Section 889 representation in the offer and the contracting officer evaluates it. But the same record does not show a public searchable equivalent for the micro-purchases that do not require active SAM registration when paid with a governmentwide commercial purchase card. (C6)

That is the finding. The records reviewed explain how the policy can move from an annual SAM representation to an offer-level representation; they do not explain where an outside reader, or a purchase-card user working outside the ordinary SAM-registration path, can search the current representation.

## What this does not show

This does not show that purchase-card transactions are exempt from Section 889. The RFO text’s purchase-card exception concerns active SAM registration, not the underlying prohibition. It also does not show that a civilian agency is noncompliant. HHS’s document demonstrates an offer-level operational path, and a missing public repository is a traceability gap, not proof that the internal records do not exist. (C7)

The next useful record would be a governmentwide or agency-specific purchase-card workflow that identifies the required Section 889 representation and where the resulting offer-level record is retained or made searchable.
