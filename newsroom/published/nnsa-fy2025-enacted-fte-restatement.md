## The finding

**Fact.** NNSA's FY2026 request for Federal Salaries and Expenses lists 1,995 federal full-time equivalents as the FY2025 enacted level. Of those, 1,974 are paid from the account and 21 through the Working Capital Fund. The enacted total on that row is $500,000,000, from a $527,169,000 gross line and $27,169,000 of prior-year balances. (C1)

**Fact.** The FY2027 justification for the same account lists 1,967 federal FTEs as the FY2025 enacted level. Of those, 1,946 are paid from the account and 21 through the Working Capital Fund. The enacted total is again $500,000,000, from a $521,410,000 gross line and $21,410,000 of prior-year balances. (C3)

**Fact.** The later total is 28 FTEs lower. The Working Capital Fund count does not change. The 28-FTE difference is inside the salaries-and-expenses rows: 1,974, then 1,946. The net appropriation does not change. The gross line is $5,759,000 lower, and prior-year balances are $5,759,000 smaller, so the $500,000,000 total is the same figure built a different way. (C5)

## Headquarters and the field

**Fact.** The office rows for that same labeled year do not match, and they do not all move in one direction. (C2, C4, C5)

Headquarters is 56 lower in the later book. The eight field offices together are 28 higher. Those two shifts produce the 28-FTE drop in the account total. (C5)

**Fact.** Neither book says the first FY2025 column was an estimate, a usage figure, or a count that had been reclassified. Both books exclude federal staff paid from the Secure Transportation Asset and Naval Reactors program-direction accounts. (C6)

## View

The number a reader should stop quoting for FY2025 is 1,995. The later official book prints 1,967 for the same account, the same year, and the same "enacted" label, and it does not say what changed. Headquarters is the large move: 1,431 becomes 1,375. That is 56 FTEs, about 3.9 percent of the earlier headquarters figure, inside a revision that lowers the whole account by only 28 because the field rows rise.

This is a record the nuclear-security oversight staff is counted in. A headquarters figure taken from the FY2026 request is already 56 FTEs away from the figure NNSA printed next. The $500 million total did not move with it, so the revision is in the staffing count, not in the size of the enacted appropriation.

## What this does not prove

The columns are funded full-time-equivalent levels. They do not identify a person hired, a person separated, or a post left empty. The field-office increase of 28 means the headquarters difference of 56 is not a 56-FTE reduction in the account. The account total falls by 28. (C5, C6)

A FY2026 request of 2,003 FTEs and a later FY2026 enacted column of 1,846 are a request and a subsequent enacted level. Each book labels that comparison as a different stage. It is not this finding.

The books give no cause for the FY2025 rewrite. A later justification could revise 1,967 again, as this one revised 1,995. (C6)
