## The finding

**Fact.** NIH's public case summaries show the number of contacted-institution cases rising from 255 as of November 16, 2023, to 263 as of March 13, 2025, and 273 as of June 12, 2026. The reports also move the associated allegation totals from 639 to 667 to 721 scientists. These are the same top-level population — cases in which NIH staff contacted an institution — measured at different cutoffs. (C1)

**Fact.** The outcome tables change alongside the population. “Any serious violation” rises from 214 cases in the 2023 report to 228 in the 2025 report and 236 in the 2026 report. “No violation” moves from 16 to 7 to 10. Repayment cases move from 32, totaling $24,076,727, to 50, totaling $36,916,316, and then 55, totaling $39,572,176. These categories describe different measures and are not additive. (C2)

## What the public record does — and does not — show

**Fact.** NIH's public decision matrix says it guides when the agency will contact an applicant or recipient and whether mitigation may be needed. It also says most allegations are closed internally without outreach to institutions. NIH's May 2026 update describes the matrix as a decision tree and describes disclosure and overlap checks for current and pending support. (C3)

**Inference.** Those materials explain why allegations received and contacted cases are different populations. They do not identify which of the 18 additional contacted cases in the three snapshots were newly added, resolved, or reclassified, nor do they connect the changes in outcome totals to individual rows. The reviewed public reports, aggregate data page, reports index, and process materials contain no row-level historical crosswalk. (C4)

That is a records-continuity boundary, not evidence that NIH's counts are wrong. The snapshots have different as-of dates, and the outcome categories overlap. A later case-level release, methodology note, or downloadable historical series could reconcile the changes.

GAO's separate review reports 697 allegations and 264 cases with disclosure violations from 2017 through 2025, but its frame is broader than NIH's contacted-institution population. Those figures cannot be added to or used to audit NIH's 255/263/273 series. (C5)

This investigation makes no claim about foreign interference, unlawful conduct, or any named researcher or institution. It tests only what a reader can reproduce from NIH's public aggregate records.
